What official material confirms

Official Kickstarter and store material describe a construction system built around freedom: you build your dream house both inside and out, with friends able to feel at home in the structures you design. The same source names more than 200 building items spanning foundations, windows, ceilings and other components.

The game runs on RedPilz Studio's in-house Systemic Engine. Official copy says every prop is interactable and reacts to weather and to classical elements such as water, fire and air. That claim is about behavior, not about placement rules.

Broader ranch construction is also confirmed in the official feature overview: house construction, animal facilities, fences, sprinklers, greenhouses and utility systems are all named as buildable ranch infrastructure.

What is not confirmed: whether placement uses a grid, which specific part satisfies each objective, and how the parts system behaves when an objective fails. None of that is published in the official material we can cite, so this guide keeps it as TBD pending a versioned in-game observation.

Official Accidental demolition refunds half the materials. The pre-EA Ranching v2 update notes state that all crafted buildings give 50% of their resources back when destroyed accidentally — a quality-of-life safety net, not a free redesign: you still lose the other half, so demolish deliberately. This is pre-EA official documentation; the exact refund behavior has not yet been independently rechecked in the current EA build.

Materials: Stone, Wood, Hay, Charcoal and Zirconite

Quick answer: use the resource guide for player-facing routes, then use the materials database for the source and evidence boundary of each material.

  • Stone: collect the loose supply on land you own first. A reliable repeatable route after that supply is exhausted is still being verified.
  • Wood Logs: current community reports obtain them from trees; exact respawn and ownership rules remain unverified.
  • Hay: confirmed chicken feed, but the public gathering route is still based on a single current-build report.
  • Charcoal: multiple current players burn Wood Logs and use a fire extinguisher before collecting the result. The retained evidence does not lock down one key binding or exact timer.

Parts, objectives and the building loop

Official copy confirms the parts catalogue and the objective-driven tutorial, but not the exact rules connecting them. The practical consequence for a new rancher is simple: do not assume a part counts toward an objective until the current build confirms it.

  • Parts catalogue: more than 200 building items including foundations, windows, ceilings and structural components.
  • Objectives: construction quests ask for specific results (for example, a roof or a set of exterior walls). Which pieces count is decided by the live build, not by the part's name alone.
  • Placement freedom: the intended system is flexible design; the current build's placement and undo behavior is what actually matters when a piece misbehaves.
  • Grid behavior: not officially confirmed. Until a current-build test records whether pieces snap to a grid, treat free-form and grid placement both as unverified.

The official store copy also lists construction among the first-week tutorial topics, so the main quest is the best place to learn what your build actually accepts.

What to record before placing

If you hit a placement problem, the evidence this site needs is a dated, versioned record of the objective and the exact pieces used.

  • Exact quest text: the objective's wording, not a paraphrase, including the requested count.
  • Blueprint names: the specific pieces placed, in order, and which were rejected.
  • Parcel state: whether the building site or parcel was purchased and its ownership state.
  • Build number: every record must name the version, because Early Access changes frequently.

Two independent current-build records under comparable conditions promote a finding to Corroborated. Until then, a single report is a useful test lead, not a settled fact.
