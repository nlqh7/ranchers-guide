Police Wanted Levels in The Ranchers: Codes, Force and Surrender Rules

Updated for Early Access 0.8.10.x (August 2026) · August 7, 2026 · Reading time: ~5 min

Evidence status: entire page sourced from one official announcement (Police District Update, published before Early Access) · individual values and edge cases may have changed in the current build · reviewed Aug 7, 2026 — How we verify →

Answer first

Official The Ranchers Police District (R.P.D) runs a 5-level wanted system. Force escalates from peaceful fine collection (Level 1, Code Green) to heavy lethal weapons (Level 5, Code Black). At any level you can surrender to reduce your sentence and bail, or try to evade: if you break line of pursuit, police cars keep searching only briefly — stay hidden and you are eventually considered evaded, your fines are wiped, and you keep the loot. Get caught and you face the maximum fines and jail time.

The 5 wanted levels, level by level

Official Every row below comes from the official "Police District Update" announcement on the Steam news hub, reviewed August 2026. That announcement predates Early Access: it is official documentation of the system, but individual values and edge cases may have changed in the current build (0.8.10.x), which has not yet been independently rechecked:

Wanted levelStatus codePolice behaviorWhat happens if they get you
Level 1 Code Green Police drones aim for a peaceful resolution — they will not arrest or attack if you pay your fines. This level handles minor offenses. Pay up and you avoid custody entirely; carry on with your day.
Level 2 Code Green No lethal force, but the police strongly encourage you to surrender. If caught — or if you surrender — you are taken into police custody, likely with a sentence.
Level 3 Code Yellow Police drones switch to non-lethal, dissuasive tactics. Getting caught means custody with a probable sentence.
Level 4 Code Red Police are now authorized to use light lethal weapons; their goal is to end the chase quickly. Custody on capture — now under live fire.
Level 5 Code Black Heavy lethal weapons are in play to end the chase at all costs; the police will do whatever it takes. The highest-force response in the game — surrender is the cheap way out.

Surrender vs. getting caught

Official At any wanted level you have a choice: surrender to reduce your sentence and bail, or push your luck evading. If you surrender or are caught, you are taken into custody and placed in a cell, where you pay your fines and potentially serve jail time depending on your crimes. The difference matters: getting caught results in the maximum fines and jail time, while surrendering at any wanted level leads to lesser fines and fewer jail days.

One multiplayer silver lining from the same announcement: while you are in custody, your friends can join your game, visit you, and take care of your crops and cattle.

Evasion: how the search ends

Official Losing the pursuers is not instantly over: police cars continue searching for you briefly. If they catch you again during that window, the chase restarts. If you manage to stay hidden for a while, you are eventually considered evaded — and a successful evasion wipes your fines and lets you keep all your loot.

So the risk/reward trade at high wanted levels is explicit: surrender early for reduced fines and bail, or gamble on hiding through the short search window for a clean slate.

Wanted level FAQ

Can I just pay my way out of a wanted level?

At Level 1, yes — paying the fine resolves it peacefully with no custody. From Level 2 upward, surrender or capture means custody, a cell, fines and possibly jail days; surrendering still reduces the sentence and bail compared with being caught.

When do the police start using lethal force?

Level 4 (Code Red) authorizes light lethal weapons and Level 5 (Code Black) brings heavy lethal weapons. Levels 1–3 are non-lethal: fine collection at Level 1, surrender pressure at Level 2, and non-lethal dissuasive tactics at Level 3.

I lost the cops — why are police cars still after me?

That is the official search behavior: police cars keep searching briefly after you break the chase, and being spotted restarts it. Stay hidden through that window and you are considered evaded — fines wiped, loot kept.

Is surrendering ever worth it at Level 5?

Per the official notes, surrendering at any wanted level — including Level 5 — leads to lesser fines and fewer jail days than being caught, while a successful evasion wipes fines entirely. The choice is reduced penalty versus all-or-nothing escape under heavy lethal force.
